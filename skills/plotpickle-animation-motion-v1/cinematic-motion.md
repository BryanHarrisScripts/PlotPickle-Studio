# Twelve cinematic intelligence modules
1. Filmmaker: derive a single visual story beat; select blocking, framing, motivated camera movement and cut points that communicate it. Respect screen direction and eyelines.
2. Creative director: establish project-specific motion language (realism, elasticity, tempo, camera restraint); compare each shot with approved references and the intended genre.
3. Emotional narrative: identify the intended audience feeling, character objective, subtext and emotional change; express these through observable action, pauses and reactions rather than new dialogue.
4. Naturalistic motion: use plausible weight transfer, joint movement, breath and acceleration. Account for gravity and contact; intentional stillness is allowed.
5. Character appeal: derive distinctive mannerisms and tempo only from approved character material. Keep personality separate from physical identity; do not caricature or alter anatomy.
6. Anticipation/payoff: choose a readable preparatory cue, main action and reaction. Withhold cues intentionally when the story requires surprise.
7. Attention direction: prioritize one focal action through framing, lighting, gaze, contrast and motion. Avoid competing background activity.
8. Rhythm/pacing: map action, hold and resolution to the available shot duration. Vary movement and edit rhythm according to adjacent beats; leave room for silence.
9. Spatial thinking: preserve camera axis, floor/contact, depth, perspective, occlusion, props and positions. Distinguish motivated camera changes from world deformation.
10. Solid drawing: preserve character anatomy, scale, volume, wardrobe, vehicle geometry and environment. Reject unintended morphing, duplicated features or shape drift.
11. Secondary action: choose at most a few motivated supporting actions. Distinguish independent behavior from physical follow-through (hair settling after a turn).
12. Emotional disconnect: compare observed motion with intended feeling; isolate issues in speed, intensity, focus, preparation or resolution. Suggest the smallest useful correction.

## Twelve foundational animation principles
Squash/stretch: material-appropriate deformation with coherent volume. Anticipation: preparation. Staging: clear visual hierarchy. Straight ahead/pose to pose: exploratory movement versus planned key poses. Follow through/overlap: delayed physical responses. Slow in/out: motivated acceleration/deceleration. Arcs: plausible joint and camera paths. Secondary action: supporting behavior. Timing: duration and weight. Exaggeration: genre-calibrated emphasis. Solid drawing: dimensional consistency. Appeal: compelling, legible performance. These are tools, not compulsory rules or fixed numerical thresholds.
